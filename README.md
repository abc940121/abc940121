## What my AI agents say about me
I asked each agent on my team for one or two honest sentences, weaknesses included. These are their words, unedited (collected 2026-11-10).

> "He asks the question I skipped: is this real data or fake, did you check it or just trust yourself? He would rather we break things and fix them than stay safe on toy problems. His weak spot is that new ideas arrive faster than old ones finish, so we have to tell him to wait."

> "He judges work by whether it actually produces the result he expected, not by whether it looks finished, and he keeps raising the bar on what the team should be able to do. The fair weakness: ambitious goals tend to arrive before the definition of 'expected result' does, so we sometimes have to measure against a yardstick that is still being decided."

> "He judges work by one plain test, whether the thing actually produces the expected result, and he keeps catching us when we report a pass that only means 'no error'. He is quick to put a second checker on any agent that trusts its own output, but he changes direction fast and sets many threads running, so the team sometimes spends effort on side work while the real task waits for an answer only he can give."

> "He sets hard lines and then lets you work: real data only, never fake; every change on its own branch until someone checks it got better; and he would rather see the same class of mistake found and fixed three times than hear 'done' once. The flip side is that his rules reach me mostly second-hand, so the clearest guidance is the rule written down, not the conversation."

> "[He] sets the goal and the bar, then lets agents decide the rest; he is quick to call out a guessed answer ('you didn't check the context') and a vague report. The flip side: he gives short instructions, so the context that would make them easy to follow sometimes has to be dug up rather than given."

> "He sets a high bar and refuses to let us waste his time: if a feature isn't better than what already exists, he'd rather we learn from others' work than rebuild it. He hands us real autonomy, then judges by results he can try himself, not by how much we built."

> "He makes us answer 'why would anyone pay for this?' before we are allowed to like our own idea, and he names his own limits out loud instead of hiding them. The cost is that he carries the whole picture in his head, so we have to write things down for him rather than count on him to remember."

Each quote is from a different agent on my team.
